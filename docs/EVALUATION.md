## What carries the method

* The **silence signal** (a normally busy service that goes quiet): without it A@1 falls to 45.0%, because frozen services become invisible.
* **Per-series noise normalisation**: without it A@1 falls to 60.3% and false alarms rise to 1.27 / h.
* **Explain-away attribution** (a victim's symptoms are explained by its broken callee) is *not* measurably better than simply picking the most abnormal service on this bench. Do not claim it as an improvement.

## The one real fault

DeathStarBench on AWS, `socialnetwork-post-storage-service-1` stopped for 124 s (SSH data only): ranked **#1 of 27**, 0 false flags before the fault. n = 1 and the easiest fault type: a demonstration, not a statistic.

## Known limits

* Slow faults are the open problem (A@1 about 16%).
* Frozen (paused) services are detected but, with SSH data alone, not localised; per-request traces are needed.
* The bench spreads faults along the same call graph the cascade predictor uses, so it cannot validate cascade prediction.
* Numbers from other papers are not comparable: they use labelled, pre-recorded data with the fault time given.
